# Bluer

An interactive artwork by **Aanya Sharma** about the ordinary things air pollution
quietly changed.

`index.html` is the whole work. One file, no build step, no dependencies, no
network requests. Open it in a browser.

## What it is

One continuous memory, told as a world that never cuts. A mother opens a bedroom
window; the window widens until it *is* the world; the curtains lengthen into
bedsheets on a line; a corner of a sheet folds into a kite; the kite climbs into
evening and becomes a star; the line in your hand becomes the line between two
stars; two stars swell into the lenses of a pair of binoculars; the lens circle
shrinks onto the crayon sun of a child's drawing; the dust off that drawing stays
in the air. Then the same places again, years later, with different air. Then the
first bedroom, where you reach for the latch and something stops you.

## How it is built

Two ideas do most of the work.

**One aperture.** There is a single shape you look through, and it travels:
window rectangle → open world → two binocular circles → one circle → a sheet of
paper → a window again. Every scene renders inside it. Because the shape is
continuous across scene boundaries, there is nothing to cross-fade — a boundary
mid-dusk simply keeps getting darker. Object-to-object morphs (curtain→sheet,
sheet→kite, kite→star, star→lens, lens→sun, crayon dust→particulate) are handled
by shared renderers whose parameters travel, not by swapping one drawing for
another.

**One atmosphere.** The entire world derives from a single number — PM2.5
concentration in µg/m³. Extinction is `σ = 3·pm + σ_rayleigh`, visual range is
Koschmieder's `3.912/σ`, and contrast falls with `exp(−σd)` over each layer's real
distance. Distant things dissolve toward *airlight* — the sky's own colour,
leaning blue — never toward a flat grey overlay. This is why the hills go before
the rooftops do, and it is also why holding your finger down in chapter three can
open a circle of the old air: the same renderer, a different number.

Everything is drawn live on one 2D canvas. Terrain, grass and the child's drawing
are painted once into offscreen buffers so the detail can be lavish, then blitted
and veiled per layer. Sound is synthesised — no audio files.

## Interaction

Scrolling moves between chapters but never performs the work. Gates hold you at a
beat until you have done something: part two curtains, turn a latch, lift a sash,
push between hanging sheets, pull a kite line to climb, trace a constellation,
hold still to focus binoculars, colour with a crayon, brush soot off washing that
keeps coming back, carry the washing indoors, breathe on cold glass, draw one
patch of blue onto a window.

Fourteen small things are hidden in the world that nothing asks you to find. The
count appears at the end.

## Accessibility

- Keyboard: arrows/WASD drive a virtual pointer, Enter satisfies the current
  action, space/PageUp/PageDown scroll.
- **Skip ▸** clears any gate without breaking the piece.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured throughout.
- **CC** captions every sound.
- A full prose description of the entire work sits in the document for screen
  readers.

## Sources

Every figure in the evidence sequence links to its origin. Claims are separated
into physics, measured, documented, and memory — and the night-sky losses are
attributed to light pollution, not to particulates, because that is what the
evidence supports.

- [WHO — Ambient (outdoor) air quality and health](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ambient-(outdoor)-air-quality-and-health)
- [WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines 2021](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034228)
- [NASA Earth Observatory — Aerosols](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/features/Aerosols)
- [US EPA — Visibility and haze](https://www.epa.gov/visibility/basic-information-about-visibility)
- [US EPA — Particulate matter basics](https://www.epa.gov/pm-pollution/particulate-matter-pm-basics)
- [Kyba et al. 2023, *Science* — star visibility](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abq7781)

## Repository layout

`index.html` is generated by concatenating `build/*` in filename order. Edit the
parts, then:

```sh
cat build/01-shell.html build/02-core.js build/03-render.js build/03b-paint.js \
    build/04-life.js build/05-sound.js build/06-scenes-a.js build/07-scenes-b.js \
    build/08-scenes-c.js build/09-evidence.js build/10-finale.js \
    build/11-director.js > index.html
printf '</script>\n</body>\n</html>\n' >> index.html
```
